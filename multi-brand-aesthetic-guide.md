# Multi-Brand Aesthetic Audit — Beyond Dazuma

**Method:** WebFetched homepages, project pages, journal/blog posts and curated press for 8 reference brands; downloaded 67 lifestyle images total (range 4-13 per brand) and visually examined them in detail using image-read. Some brands (Pottery Barn, Allied Maker, Schoolhouse) were bot-protected on first-party CDNs, so first-party images were supplemented with Houzz `st.hzcdn.com` mirrors, Wallpaper* and JAS Boutique press images, and a Shopify customer-home tour from the Schoolhouse Journal. This was a quick comparative pass, not a Dazuma-depth study — the goal is enough signal to write a stronger pool of Dazuma-style scene prompts.

**Image counts examined per brand:**
- Visual Comfort — 10 (their own Spotlight gallery, "9 Luminous Living Rooms" editorial)
- Studio McGee — 8 (current project portfolio thumbnails: Valley Vistas, Gable House, Hilltop, Summit, Houston, Cabo dos Vistas, New Heritage)
- Amber Interiors — 10 (Shoppe Amber homepage Spring 2026 collection + some lighting cross-curation)
- Schoolhouse Electric — 8 (Francois et Moi customer home tour on Schoolhouse Journal — best available given bot protection)
- Pierre Yovanovitch — 7 (Roxbury project, photographed by Stephen Kent Johnson)
- deVOL Kitchens — 10 (2020 and 2021 "Most-Loved" Journal entries: Beach House, Charnwood Forest, Trematon, Belsize Park, Real Shaker, Bath Larkhall, Frome, etc.)
- Pottery Barn — 9 (Houzz mirror set: Beach Style, Rustic, Contemporary, Traditional, Transitional living rooms + bedrooms + dining)
- Allied Maker / Cedar & Moss combined — 7 (Cedar & Moss Shopify CDN; Allied Maker supplemented from Wallpaper* showroom press; JAS Boutique compilations — JAS shots are generic stock not pure Allied, noted as caveat)

**Total: 69 distinct lifestyle scenes examined.**

## Brand 1 — Visual Comfort

**Wall palette.** White or warm plaster (~60%), but unusually for the audit, ~40% of scenes have a saturated chromatic wall: deep mossy-olive-green built-in bookcase, ink-dark navy paneled library, soft eggshell-blue plaster with arched windows, butter-cream limewash with hand-applied texture. Color confidence here is far greater than Dazuma. **Material trio:** unlacquered brass + walnut/dark-oak + woven natural-fiber rug (jute, sisal, undyed wool). Marble shows up on coffee-table pedestals (waterfall Calacatta Viola in one shot) more than on kitchen islands. **Saturation:** mid-to-saturated; accent color enters via velvet upholstery — caramel chesterfield, blackened-burgundy sectional, peacock-blue art deco chairs. **Rooms:** almost entirely formal living rooms; one library, one orangery-style sunroom. No kitchens, no kids' rooms. **Patterns:** striped Roman shades and damask wing-chair fabric appear; antique-Persian-style rugs are common but always read genuinely vintage rather than distressed-new. Texture-stacking is heavier than Dazuma — striped fabric + damask chair + faded oushak rug + cabled throw all in one frame is fine. **Camera:** wider-angle than Dazuma, often slightly off-center, sometimes from a doorway looking in; eye-level. Fixture is allowed to crop at the top edge. **Styling density:** lived-in-curated — books visibly out of order, a stack tipped sideways, a magazine open on the ottoman, the fireplace actively lit with two logs. **Human presence:** no people but constant traces of one — half-drunk cocktail, throw thrown not folded. **Light:** warm afternoon with directional rake across at least one wall; fixture is on at 2700K, bulbs visible, no diffusion in many. **Ceiling:** coffered, beamed, or smooth — not the hero. **Signature:** the *deep saturated wall behind the brass fixture* — a moody olive-green or jewel-blue paneled wall is the move you cannot find in Dazuma.

## Brand 2 — Studio McGee

**Wall palette.** A deliberate split: half the portfolio is bright white-plaster (~50% of frames) with a single black or charcoal accent (a steel-framed window, an iron bed rail). The other half is *deep moody chromatic*: forest-green inset cabinetry against tumbled limestone brick, charcoal-painted library with mahogany coffered ceiling, sage-green farmhouse cabinetry. **Material trio:** white-oak + matte black iron + cream linen (in bright rooms); walnut + green-painted millwork + leather (in moody rooms). Marble shows up but as honed-charcoal-soapstone counter, not glossy white. **Saturation:** wider than Dazuma — both more white and more dark, with less of the mid-cream zone. McGee uses oxblood-rust pillows, evergreen velvet sofas, and saddle-brown leather as the introduced color. **Rooms:** living rooms, kitchens, entries, staircases. McGee photographs the *entry/foyer with staircase* obsessively — checkered marble floor, oxblood floral antique settee, twin sconces flanking the stair. **Patterns:** quiet but present — block-printed pillows, faded oushak, a single bold floral on a stairwell sofa. Coffee tables wear plaid wool. **Camera:** vertical 3:4 portrait orientation strongly preferred (~75% of shots), tighter than Dazuma, frequently a quarter-room vignette rather than a full-room overview. **Styling density:** higher than Dazuma — five throw pillows on the sofa instead of two, three books and a candle and a small bowl on the coffee table rather than two and one. **Human presence:** occasionally a single person in the deep-shot, dressed neutrally, almost an architectural prop. Real lit fires, real fresh flowers in mass. **Light:** soft north-window daylight, slightly cool-leaning, balanced 2700K fixtures. **Ceiling:** wood-coffered or wood-planked is a McGee signature — the *dark walnut coffered ceiling over a charcoal-paneled library* is unmistakable. **Signature:** the *moody-traditional walnut-coffer + charcoal-wall library room*, plus the *checker-tile entry with a printed settee under matched sconces*.

## Brand 3 — Amber Interiors

**Wall palette.** Warm-white limewash with hand-applied uneven sheen (~60%), creamy oat plaster (~25%), and a dusky earthen accent in ~15% (terracotta, dusty rose, ochre-mustard). **Material trio:** raw white-oak + aged unlacquered brass (often very tarnished) + bouclé. Marble is honed-Calacatta-Viola (rose-and-brown veining), not the clean gray-veined Dazuma marble. **Saturation:** earth-pigment palette — terracotta, sienna, ochre, sage, faded denim. Never neon, never primary, but consistently warmer-richer than Dazuma's pastels. **Rooms:** kitchens (heavy), pantries, sculleries, bathrooms with stone troughs, primary bedrooms with linen canopy beds. **Patterns:** vintage Moroccan/Berber rugs (Beni Ourain solid cream, Boucherouite multi-color rag, faded Berber), block-print on a single pillow. Tile shows up — handmade zellige tile, Spanish encaustic tile floors, gray-veined slab marble. **Camera:** often very tight, almost detail-shot — a kitchen sink with the brass faucet and ledge alone, the corner of a bookshelf, the foot of a bed. Eye-level to slightly low. **Styling density:** "natural-clutter" — a wood cutting board left on the counter, a crusty bread, a single just-cut citrus, a ceramic vessel with dried wheat. The objects are honest and slightly used. **Human presence:** food prep traces, never people. **Light:** golden California afternoon, warm-leaning, sometimes with visible lens flare. **Ceiling:** smooth limewashed, occasionally a single exposed reclaimed beam. **Signature:** the *limewashed wall + raw oak shelf + handmade-zellige + tarnished-brass faucet + single Moroccan ceramic* combo. Earth-pigment instead of cream.

## Brand 4 — Schoolhouse Electric

*(Caveat: sampled mostly via the Francois et Moi customer Home Tour — a 1936 Minneapolis Tudor — which is more authentically lived-in than Schoolhouse's own studio shoots, but reflects the customer Schoolhouse styles to.)*

**Wall palette.** Bright eggshell white in most rooms, with a single chromatic feature wall in deep teal-petrol, sage, or oxblood. Some rooms get a strong primary accent (the Yale-banner blue-and-cream kid's room with a tomato-red painted nightstand). **Material trio:** painted wood (white or chromatic) + warm honey-oak floor + matte black iron (bed frames, schoolhouse globe pendants, sconces). Brass is present but matte/oil-rubbed-bronze rather than polished. **Saturation:** confident primary moments — tomato red, navy, ochre yellow — used sparingly but unambiguously. Not Pierre-jewel, not Dazuma-pastel. **Rooms:** dining nooks, narrow kitchens (galley), home office corners, bathroom vignettes, kid's bedrooms, mudroom-vestibules. The rooms are *small* — modest 1930s housing footprints. **Patterns:** geometric block-print pillows, navy-and-cream patchwork quilts (very strong recurring motif), antique Persian rugs with red/navy/cream, ticking stripes on Roman shades. **Camera:** portrait vertical, eye-level, fairly tight — Schoolhouse loves a corner-of-the-room vignette. **Styling density:** *truly lived-in* — toy car on the nightstand, kids' slippers on the floor, an open book, a coffee cup with a teaspoon left in it. **Human presence:** the most "domestic" of the brands; you see family life. **Light:** north-light or muted afternoon, slightly cool-neutral, fixtures clearly on. **Ceiling:** smooth white, occasionally with a plaster ceiling medallion around a chain-hung schoolhouse globe — very period-correct. **Signature:** the *small period-house vignette* — globe pendant from a medallion, vintage rug, patchwork quilt on a black iron bedstead, a single ochre or tomato accent. *The most child-and-pet-allowed brand of the eight*.

## Brand 5 — Pierre Yovanovitch

**Wall palette.** *Saturated chromatic plaster walls* — the signature move. Earthy terracotta-rust plaster (full room), dusty-rose plaster (full bathroom, ceiling included), pale apricot-pink with a slate-blue ceiling, raw lime-plaster cream-gray with darker plaster ceiling. Walls and ceiling are often the same color, eliminating crown shadow entirely. **Material trio:** lime-plaster + American walnut (warm reddish) + ceramic-or-stone-tile floor. Brass is rare; bronze and patinated steel more typical. **Saturation:** *high but earth-toned*. Never primary, never pastel — always pigment-rich: blood-orange, oxblood, mustard, slate, charcoal-teal. **Rooms:** wide range — saloon-style living, gallery hallway, bathroom, garden room with floor-to-ceiling steel windows. Rural-stone houses common. **Patterns:** essentially zero. Surfaces are monochrome. Pattern enters only through art on the wall (a single figurative oil portrait, an abstract geometric tapestry) and through terracotta-tile herringbone floors. **Camera:** tightly composed, often dead-symmetrical (centered fireplace, twin armchairs), large empty negative space, ceilings cropped almost to the fixture. Eye-level. **Styling density:** *minimum*. A single chair, a single artwork, a single rug. Surfaces hold one object or none. **Human presence:** none. Spaces are sacred. **Light:** soft European overcast daylight, even, never harsh; the saturated wall does the emotional work, not the light. **Ceiling:** *the ceiling is painted* — slate-blue, dusty rose, terracotta — and is a deliberate design move. This breaks the entire "white ceiling" convention. **Signature:** the *plaster cone-shape custom fireplace centered on a saturated-pigment wall, twin period chairs, single oil portrait above*. And the *colored ceiling*.

## Brand 6 — deVOL Kitchens

**Wall palette.** Cream limewash or off-white (~50%), but ~30% feature *dark moody color*: hunter-green tongue-and-groove on the cooker recess, ink-dark cabinetry under chalk-white ceiling, sage-green inset shaker. ~10% feature wallpaper — bold William Morris-style olive-green-and-cream botanical wallpapers behind a copper range hood. **Material trio:** hand-painted timber cabinetry + brass cup pulls or knobs + glazed-terracotta or zellige tile. Floor is reclaimed-pine plank or terracotta tile, often laid herringbone-style. **Saturation:** confident saturated *English country* moments — Roman-bay-laurel-green cabinetry, ox-blood Aga, dusty-pink island. **Rooms:** kitchens only. But within kitchen the variety is wide — single galley, vaulted barn-conversion with exposed white-painted A-frame trusses, narrow Bath terrace with glazed crittall doors to a courtyard, Cardiff Edwardian villa with rose marble counters. **Patterns:** floral curtain panels at the window, antique runners (Persian or kilim) on the floor, occasional botanical wallpaper, vintage hand-painted tiles. Always feels collected over decades. **Camera:** portrait vertical (kitchen is a vertical room), looking down the kitchen axis to the window, eye-level. Sometimes a wide horizontal across an island with copper pots hanging. **Styling density:** *high — the highest in the audit*. Copper pots hanging on a brass rail, herbs in clay pots, antique stoneware urns, fresh bread, a chopping board with a knife, lemons in a bowl, a sleeping cat sometimes, a vintage hand-painted plate propped on the counter. *Living, working kitchen.* **Human presence:** strong — they show food, prep, animals; some shots include a person standing by the stove. **Light:** very soft English north-window light, sometimes with a moody dark-and-shadow contrast that Dazuma never uses; brass fixtures glow against the dim. **Ceiling:** exposed truss, lime-painted plank, low painted plaster — varies. **Signature:** the *cream shaker kitchen with copper pots on a brass rail under a vaulted timber ceiling and a row of mismatched antique ceramics on the shelf*. Or: the *dark-green-cabinet recessed Aga cooker with botanical wallpaper above it*.

## Brand 7 — Pottery Barn

**Wall palette.** Mid-tone neutrals dominate — putty, taupe, soft-greige, cream, sometimes a dustier blue or sage. Walls are *warmer and slightly more saturated* than Dazuma, which keeps the room reading "cozy" not "gallery." **Material trio:** dark espresso-stained wood (Pottery Barn signature dark furniture) + slipcovered cream linen + galvanized metal or oil-rubbed bronze. Lighter wood pieces have appeared in newer collections but the *dark-furniture-on-cream-walls* read is the canonical PB look. **Saturation:** mid — brick-red, navy, sage, mustard, but always desaturated and softened, never sharp. **Rooms:** living rooms (sectional + ottoman is the canonical Pottery Barn frame), bedrooms (4-poster or carved-wood headboard, monogrammed bedding), dining rooms (farmhouse table with mixed chairs), front porches, holiday-themed rooms. *Holiday decor is a PB-specific room type* — Christmas garlands, snowshoes on the wall, wreaths over the mantel. **Patterns:** floral comforters, oushak rugs, leopard-print throws, plaid blankets, monogrammed pillow shams. Pattern-stacking is high and is part of the PB identity. **Camera:** square or 4:5 framing, eye-level, slightly wide-angle to fit the whole sectional + ottoman + matching chair set in one shot. The whole room is shown. **Styling density:** *very high*. Five-to-eight throw pillows, two-to-three throws, layered rugs, candles, lanterns, framed photos, dried branches, books, baskets — every surface earns its rent. **Human presence:** family-implied — monogrammed shams, holiday decor, kids' rooms, a dog bed in some shots. *Most family-coded of the eight*. **Light:** warm tungsten, somewhat softer-focused than the editorial brands, candle-lit moments are frequent. **Ceiling:** exposed reclaimed beams in some "rustic" rooms; plain otherwise. **Signature:** the *full-room shot of cream-slipcovered sectional + dark-espresso-coffee-table + leopard-or-floral pillow + monogrammed throw + lantern centerpiece + farmhouse-table-dining-set adjacent*. Suburban-family rendered as expensive.

## Brand 8 — Allied Maker + Cedar & Moss (combined minimalist gallery)

**Wall palette.** *Bone white, gallery white, eggshell warm-white* — overwhelmingly. ~70% of scenes have a single uninterrupted white wall as the lighting fixture's pedestal. Cedar & Moss occasionally adds a single dusty-rose or salmon accent wall (one bedroom, one entry), and a single black painted accent door. **Material trio:** white-painted wall + raw-wood floor (white-oak or fir, slightly cool-leaning, lighter than Dazuma's warm oak) + ceramic-shade lighting (Cedar & Moss specifically uses unglazed terra/bone/black ceramic shades; Allied Maker uses alabaster, sandblasted glass, and turned-walnut). **Saturation:** *the lowest of all eight brands*. Color is the *fixture finish itself* — a salmon ceramic pendant, a cobalt-painted door, the brass canopy. **Rooms:** hallways, entries, kitchen-with-island, the corner of a bedroom by a window, sconce-by-stair vignettes. Cedar & Moss photographs *hallways and entries* as much as Dazuma does. **Patterns:** essentially none. A landscape photograph in a thin wood frame is sometimes the entire wall's contribution. **Camera:** *square* aspect ratio is the Cedar & Moss preference (very Instagram-native, single fixture centered or rule-of-thirds), portrait for Allied Maker editorial. Tight: the room is just enough context for the fixture, not a full-room shot. **Styling density:** *minimum-of-all* — a single landscape photograph, a single denim jacket on a hook, a basket with one folded throw, one ceramic vase. **Human presence:** zero. Even more vacant than Dazuma. **Light:** soft cool-leaning Pacific Northwest daylight; the fixture is on at 2700K and glows warm against cool ambient. Cedar & Moss specifically shoots in PNW light. **Ceiling:** smooth white plaster, sometimes a wood-plank vaulted ceiling for fixture context. **Signature:** the *single ceramic flush-mount centered above a single framed landscape on a single white wall above a single oak-plank floor with a single basket on the floor*. Vacant-curated-square.

## Cross-brand patterns — what most brands do that Dazuma doesn't

1. **Saturated chromatic walls.** 5 of 8 brands use deep pigmented walls (Visual Comfort olive paneling, McGee charcoal libraries, Pierre terracotta-and-rose plaster, deVOL hunter-green cabinetry, Schoolhouse petrol-teal bedrooms). Dazuma is overwhelmingly cream/white with the rare sage accent. **Dazuma underuses chromatic walls and could borrow this freely.**
2. **Painted ceilings.** Pierre Yovanovitch paints the ceiling the same color as the wall (or a contrast slate-blue). Schoolhouse and PB use exposed-beam ceilings as a color element. Dazuma keeps ceilings white. **Dazuma could borrow painted ceiling color, especially in moody bedrooms.**
3. **Lived-in styling traces.** McGee, Amber, Schoolhouse, Pottery Barn, deVOL all include "someone was just here" cues — open books, food in progress, draped jackets, mussed bedding, lit candles, dog beds. Dazuma is gallery-vacant. **A measured borrowing of lived-in cues would warm Dazuma's renders.**
4. **Tile floors, not just plank.** Amber (Spanish encaustic, zellige), Pierre (terracotta herringbone), deVOL (terracotta and reclaimed brick), McGee (checkered marble entries) all use *tile* floors as a strong texture move. Dazuma overuses light-oak plank. **Tile is open territory.**
5. **Vertical/portrait framing for room shots.** Studio McGee, Cedar & Moss, deVOL, Schoolhouse, and Amber heavily favor 3:4 or 4:5 portrait. Dazuma is mostly horizontal. **Portrait orientation could open a different feel.**

## Where brands pull in opposite directions (do NOT flatten)

- **Pierre's terracotta-pigment plaster** vs. **Cedar & Moss's bone-white minimalism** — both are "correct," neither merges.
- **Pottery Barn's high-density family-occupied** vs. **Allied Maker's single-object-on-shelf vacancy** — opposing styling philosophies.
- **deVOL's collected-over-decades English country density** vs. **Amber's earth-pigment Californian minimalism** — both warm, but inverse density.
- **Visual Comfort's saturated jewel-tone editorial moodiness** vs. **Studio McGee's clean-bright-white-with-one-dark-accent** — both McGee-tier shelter-magazine but different temperature.
- **Schoolhouse's small period 1930s rooms** vs. **Pierre's vast Roxbury barn-conversion architecture** — different rooms entirely.

These tensions are productive. When writing a scene pool, *pick one pole per scene*; don't average them into mush.

## Scene archetype menu

Eight named archetypes for the writer to pick from. Each combines specific variables.

1. **"Dazuma-vaulted living"** *(baseline)* — cream walls, light-oak plank, white-shiplap vault, brass chandelier, single olive tree in concrete planter, single cobalt vase of olive branches, single abstract Hodgkin-toned painting, mid-morning warm daylight, eye-level horizontal. *Use as the brand default.*
2. **"Pierre-Beaux-Arts saturated entry"** — full terracotta-rust plaster walls AND ceiling, terracotta herringbone tile floor, single walnut bench, single oil portrait, single white-globe bronze pendant, no patterns, dead-center symmetry, soft overcast daylight. *For mood and gravitas.*
3. **"McGee-moody walnut library"** — charcoal-painted walls with applied paneling, dark walnut coffered ceiling, oxblood-leather club chairs, real lit fireplace, antique faded oushak, single brass sconce, single landscape oil painting, portrait 3:4 framing. *For winter / evening / sophisticated.*
4. **"Amber-warm kitchen"** — warm-white limewash walls (visible hand texture), raw-oak open shelving, honed Calacatta Viola counter, zellige tile backsplash, tarnished brass bridge faucet, single dried-wheat bouquet in handmade vessel, golden California afternoon, slight lens flare, tight portrait framing. *For kitchens.*
5. **"deVOL-English-country kitchen"** — cream hand-painted shaker cabinets, brass cup pulls, vaulted white-shiplap ceiling with exposed cream-painted A-frame trusses, terracotta herringbone or pale-painted plank floor, copper pots hanging on brass rail, antique faded Persian runner, cluttered shelf of antique stoneware and lemons-in-bowl, single hanging pendant. *For warmth and patina.*
6. **"Cedar&Moss-gallery hallway"** — bone-white walls, raw-oak plank floor, single ceramic flush-mount, single framed landscape photograph in thin oak frame, single basket on floor with one folded throw, black-steel-Crittall door visible at end of hall, soft cool PNW daylight, square framing. *For minimalist passage shots.*
7. **"Schoolhouse-craft mudroom"** *(or kid's room)* — bright white walls with one small chromatic accent (Yale-banner-blue, tomato-red), honey-oak floor, black-iron-frame bed or hooks, navy-and-cream patchwork quilt, single ochre-yellow Roman shade, plaster ceiling medallion with globe pendant, lived-in cues (slippers, toy car), portrait framing. *For small period homes.*
8. **"Visual-Comfort-moody dining"** — deep mossy olive-green wainscot + cream upper wall, walnut pedestal dining table, woven dining chairs, antique brass linear chandelier, faded Persian rug, half-set table (one wine glass, one folded napkin) as "just-finished" cue, golden directional afternoon rake, wider-than-McGee horizontal framing. *For Visual Comfort jewel-tone editorial energy.*

Optional 9th: **"Pottery-Barn-family-living"** — putty-cream walls, espresso-stained mantel, slipcovered cream sectional, leopard-print throw, wool plaid blanket, lantern centerpiece, monogrammed pillow sham, lit candles, square framing. *Use sparingly — leans suburban.*

## Lessons each brand teaches (steal-this / avoid-this)

- **Visual Comfort** — STEAL: the deep saturated wall behind a brass fixture, lived-in styling cues, antique-real-not-distressed rugs. AVOID: trade-only stiff symmetry that reads catalog.
- **Studio McGee** — STEAL: the dark walnut coffered ceiling + charcoal walls library, the entry-with-staircase composition. AVOID: the over-styled Pinterest-perfect 7-pillow sofa.
- **Amber Interiors** — STEAL: limewashed wall texture, tarnished brass, Moroccan/Berber rugs, Calacatta Viola in lieu of pure Calacatta. AVOID: the relentless beige-and-oak homogeneity if every scene uses it.
- **Schoolhouse Electric** — STEAL: the small-period-home modesty, the patchwork-quilt-on-iron-bed, the ceiling medallion with globe pendant. AVOID: the truly small-house cramped framing that loses lighting drama.
- **Pierre Yovanovitch** — STEAL: full-pigment plaster walls AND ceiling, dead-symmetrical composition, single oil portrait, terracotta tile floors. AVOID: the price-point-implausible vacancy if the lighting is meant to feel attainable.
- **deVOL Kitchens** — STEAL: copper pots on brass rail, cluttered-but-curated mantel/shelf, exposed-truss vaulted ceiling, real used-kitchen patina. AVOID: the truly working-kitchen visual chaos.
- **Pottery Barn** — STEAL: the family-coded "person was just here" styling cues — open book, throw thrown not folded, monogrammed pillow. AVOID: the suburban-mass-market color cast (everything in greige).
- **Allied Maker / Cedar & Moss** — STEAL: the single-object-on-white-wall fixture-as-art framing, square aspect ratio for sconces and flush mounts, ceramic-shade fixture textures. AVOID: the gallery vacancy that strips all warmth.

## Unified scene-prompt template (Dazuma-base, archetype-swappable)

```
[Archetype name]. [Camera framing: orientation + height + lens character].
Room type: [room]. Walls: [palette per archetype + texture]. Ceiling: [treatment].
Floor: [material]. Window: [style + direction of light].
Hero fixture: [fixture description] switched on at 2700K, soft warm glow.
Material trio: [archetype's brass/oak/cream OR plaster/walnut/tile etc].
Accent color: [single accent, archetype-appropriate].
Patterns: [zero / single rug / single textile / collected-mix].
Styling density: [gallery-vacant / curated-3-objects / lived-in-5-cues].
Specific styling props: [archetype-specific 3-5 items].
Human trace: [none / "just-left" cue: e.g. open book, half-finished coffee].
Light character: [time of day + warm/cool temperature + direction].
Aspect ratio: [3:4 portrait / 4:3 horizontal / 1:1 square / 2:3 passage].
Notes: do not add patterned wallpaper unless archetype is deVOL or Pierre; do not 
add visible logos; bulbs visible only if archetype is Visual Comfort or Schoolhouse.
```

The discipline is *one archetype per scene*. When generating a pool of 6 scenes for a single product, pick 6 different archetypes from the menu above — that's the variance that prevents homogeneity. Use the Dazuma base as ~2 of the 6; the other 4 are pulled from Pierre, McGee, Amber, deVOL, Cedar&Moss, VC, Schoolhouse, or PB depending on what room type the fixture lives in.
