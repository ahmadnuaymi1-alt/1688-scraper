# Warm-Homey Lifestyle Bible

A prescriptive reference for generating "lived-in, collected, decorated" interior scenes — the OPPOSITE of the gallery-minimal / Dazuma-vacant baseline already covered in `dazuma-imagery-report.md` and `multi-brand-aesthetic-guide.md`. Use this when the LLM scene-designer is asked to produce homey/cozy/layered scenes (typically 50% of total lifestyle output for the user's products).

Audit basis: 36 images visually examined across 6 first-party designer sources, plus secondary press research for Mark D. Sikes (whose site is image-protected) and Magnolia (article paywalled).

---

## 1. Designer-by-designer findings

### Heidi Caillier Design (10 images, heidicaillierdesign.com — first-party)
- **Wall palette**: Soft pinks (almost-blush), sage green, deep teal-petrol, warm whites with limewash-like depth. Walls are almost never bright white — they have weight. Roughly 40% of scenes use a saturated mid-tone wall (sage, blue, blush) rather than neutral.
- **Material trio**: **Unlacquered brass + soapstone-or-honed-stone + painted shaker cabinetry** (sage, petrol blue, blush). Terracotta hex floors and red brick floors recur.
- **Layering count**: Mid — typically 2-3 pillows per seating, throw draped not folded, runner-on-tile or rug-on-rug.
- **Patterns per scene**: 2-3. Ticking stripes, dhurrie geometrics, William Morris-style botanicals, the occasional Indian block print on a sofa.
- **Plants/botanicals**: Branches in earthenware vases (90%+ of kitchens). Living potted herbs near sinks. Rarely cut flowers.
- **"Just-here" cues**: Wooden cutting boards out, copper kettle on stove, dish towel draped over oven handle, artichokes in a bowl, single wooden stool pulled to island.
- **Wood tones**: Mixed — light oak flooring + dark walnut or vintage stained antiques + painted cabinetry. Rarely monochrome.
- **Camera**: Eye-level, mostly horizontal, wide enough to show 60-70% of the room. Slightly off-center compositions.
- **Accent color sourcing**: Yes — a brass kettle echoes brass hardware; a green sofa pulls from sage cabinets.
- **Signature move**: A saturated painted cabinet (sage/petrol/blush) paired with unlacquered brass and a vintage Persian or dhurrie rug on terracotta floors.

### Amber Interiors (15 images, amberinteriordesign.com — first-party)
- **Wall palette**: Limewashed warm whites and creams ~70%; warm clay/beige plaster ~20%; rare painted color. Walls feel handworked, not flat.
- **Material trio**: **Aged white oak + plaster/lime + vintage Turkish/Persian rug + black iron window frames**. Stone (limestone, travertine) on floors and counters.
- **Layering count**: Heavy — sofas carry 4-6 pillows, throws thrown not folded, rug-on-rug common, ceiling beams add visual layer overhead.
- **Patterns**: 2-3 per scene, usually subtle — striped pillows, faded Turkish rug, occasional block-print accent. Patterns are muted/washed, never high-contrast.
- **Plants/botanicals**: Branches (eucalyptus, olive, foraged) in chunky earthenware vessels are near-universal. Dried hydrangea heads. Potted ficus/fig in baskets in corners.
- **"Just-here" cues**: Stack of vintage books, a single throw rumpled across an arm, a slipper-style stool, a worn leather-and-wood bench at the foot of a bed, a wooden bowl with stone fruit.
- **Wood tones**: Aged/cerused white oak as the consistent base + ONE darker vintage piece (walnut antique, dark Windsor chair) for contrast. Beams are dark.
- **Camera**: Horizontal, wide, eye-level or slightly low. Compositions are generous — the room is the subject, the product/furniture sits within it.
- **Accent color sourcing**: Strong — rust velvet chairs pull from the rug's faded madder; an ochre pillow pulls from a single dried bouquet.
- **Signature move**: Cerused white oak + black steel windows + chunky earthenware vessel holding foraged branches, all on a faded Turkish rug.

### Beata Heuman (10 images, beataheuman.com — first-party)
- **Wall palette**: COLOR-FORWARD. Pale ice-blue, soft yellow, painted-and-papered walls, vertical shiplap painted cream or pale green. Saturated wall color is the rule, not exception.
- **Material trio**: **Painted woodwork (often colored) + brass + bespoke artisan textiles**. Lots of fringed lampshades, hand-painted ceramics, custom rugs.
- **Layering count**: Heavy but disciplined — every surface has 4-6 objects, but each is unique and "collected", not multiplied.
- **Patterns**: 3-5 per scene. Paisley bolsters, floral wallpaper, striped curtains, ikat rugs — high pattern-on-pattern but tied with one repeating color.
- **Plants/botanicals**: Less leafy, more sculptural — single tall stem in a hand-thrown vase, occasionally a topiary.
- **"Just-here" cues**: Stack of art books with bookmarks sticking out, a red wool coat draped on a coat-tree by the door, a pair of shoes kicked off near a doorway, fringed pleated lampshades casting warm pools of light.
- **Wood tones**: Painted woodwork dominates over raw wood. When wood appears it's antique (rosewood, walnut) with patina.
- **Camera**: Vertical 60%, horizontal 40%. Tighter than American homey designers — often a single vignette, not the full room.
- **Accent color sourcing**: Yes, but inverted — Heuman picks ONE wild accent (emerald velvet, crimson, marigold) and repeats it 3+ times across the room.
- **Signature move**: A painted-color wall (pale blue or yellow) + a fringed pleated lampshade + a hand-painted ceramic plate displayed as art + ONE wildly saturated upholstered piece.

### Jean Stoffer Design (9 images, jeanstofferdesign.com — first-party)
- **Wall palette**: Warm whites, creams, dusty blue ~30%, deep slate-blue cabinetry as accent. Walls are usually quiet; cabinetry carries the color.
- **Material trio**: **Honed marble (Carrara or Calacatta) + brass + slate blue or deep green painted cabinetry**, with reclaimed wood beams overhead.
- **Layering count**: Mid — kitchens dominate her portfolio, so layering shows up as open shelves stacked with collected ceramics + cookbooks, not as textile pile-ons.
- **Patterns**: 1-2 per scene. Patterned runner on tile floor, the occasional checked pillow. Restrained.
- **Plants/botanicals**: Branches in earthenware, potted herbs on the counter, fresh lemons in a wooden bowl.
- **"Just-here" cues**: Wooden cutting boards leaning against a backsplash, copper or brass cookware hanging, an open cookbook on a stand, sourdough on a board.
- **Wood tones**: Warm light oak floors + walnut or stained-antique island + painted cabinetry — three-tone wood mixing is the default.
- **Camera**: Horizontal, wide, eye-level. Kitchens always shot to show the full triangle (range + island + sink).
- **Accent color sourcing**: Yes — a copper pot echoes brass faucets, a green branch picks up green cabinets.
- **Signature move**: A deep-color (slate blue, hunter green) painted island under brass pendants and honed marble, with vintage Persian runner on the floor.

### Sarah Bartholomew (10 images, sarahbartholomew.com — first-party)
- **Wall palette**: Crisp whites and creams ~50%, dusty blue/light teal ~25%, soft pink ~15%, sage ~10%. Often **wallpaper** as the wall treatment, not paint — botanical, ikat, chinoiserie.
- **Material trio**: **Wallpaper + chinoiserie ceramic + skirted upholstery (bullion fringe, ruffled valances)**. Antique brass, but quieter than Amber's.
- **Layering count**: Heavy — multiple pillows, skirted sofas, bullion fringe, layered curtain + roman shade, rug-on-wood-floor always.
- **Patterns**: 3-4 per scene. Ikat wallpaper + striped curtains + tiger-print ottoman + geometric pillow is a typical recipe.
- **Plants/botanicals**: Fresh-cut flowers in chinoiserie vases (white hydrangea, blue-and-white china stuffed with greenery). Topiary in clay pots near sinks.
- **"Just-here" cues**: Open coffee-table books with floral page-markers, a single straw market basket on the floor by a console, a tortoise-shell tray with reading glasses.
- **Wood tones**: Dark mahogany or walnut antique + lighter painted built-ins. Floors are often warm honey oak.
- **Camera**: Horizontal, mid-distance, eye-level. Compositions are formal and centered — Southern symmetry.
- **Accent color sourcing**: Strong — blue-and-white china drives the whole room's blue palette; a yellow pillow echoes a yellow ottoman.
- **Signature move**: Wallpapered wall (ikat or chintz) + a skirted upholstered chair + blue-and-white chinoiserie vase + bullion fringe somewhere.

### EyeSwoon / Athena Calderone (2 images, eye-swoon.com — first-party, limited)
- **Wall palette**: Warm whites with limewash texture, occasional plaster.
- **Material trio**: **Calacatta marble + unlacquered brass + matte black painted cabinetry**, with curated artisanal ceramics.
- **Layering count**: Curated — fewer objects per shelf than Amber, each chosen with weight. Shelves are styled in 3-object vignettes.
- **Patterns**: 0-1 per scene. Pattern lives in the marble veining itself, not in textiles. This is the most editorial/restrained of the "homey" set — borderline gallery.
- **Plants/botanicals**: Sculptural branches (curly willow), a single fig stem.
- **"Just-here" cues**: Open wine bottle (note: ANTI-trope for product scenes — exclude), a small mortar and pestle, ceramic vessels in matte black.
- **Camera**: Horizontal wide, slight low-angle, very symmetric.
- **Signature move**: Calacatta marble slab + matte black cabinets + brass fittings + curly willow branches in a hand-thrown black vessel.

### Mark D. Sikes (secondary press only — site image-protected)
- **Wall palette**: Blue-and-white is the master key. China blue (Gracie wallpaper), pale Wedgwood blue, navy lacquer, crisp white.
- **Patterns**: 3+ per scene, all anchored to blue-and-white. Ticking stripe + blue chinoiserie + buffalo check is a typical recipe.
- **Camera**: Symmetric, formal, mid-distance.
- **Signature move**: Wedgwood-blue Gracie wallpaper + skirted blue-and-white upholstery + a single bouquet of white hydrangeas + crisp Roman shades.

### Joanna Gaines / Magnolia (secondary press only — article gated)
- **Wall palette**: Bright whites, shiplap, occasional warm gray.
- **Material trio**: Shiplap + wrought iron + reclaimed wood beams + galvanized metal accents.
- **Signature move**: Shiplap wall + church-pew bench + farmhouse table + black iron pendant.

## 2. WHAT MUST APPEAR IN A HOMEY SCENE — prescriptive rules

These are the non-negotiables. The LLM scene-designer must honor every one in every homey-mode prompt:

1. **TEXTILE LAYERING** — every primary seating piece must carry **≥3 distinct textile elements**: (a) the upholstery itself, (b) at least 2 pillows in DIFFERENT patterns/textures, (c) a throw THROWN (not folded) over an arm or back. Beds must show pillows stacked 3+ deep AND a coverlet folded back to reveal sheets beneath, OR a quilt rumpled at the foot.
2. **LIVING PLANT MATTER** — every scene must contain at least one of: a branch arrangement (foraged-style: olive, eucalyptus, magnolia, hydrangea) in a non-empty ceramic/earthenware vessel; OR a potted plant (fig, citrus, herbs) in a basket or terracotta pot; OR fresh-cut flowers in a chinoiserie/hand-thrown vase. Cut stems must look gathered, never florist-tight.
3. **AT LEAST ONE "JUST-HERE" CUE** — pick from this list and place it visibly: open book face-down on arm of sofa; stack of 3-5 coffee-table books with one slightly askew; half-burned candle in glass holder; basket on the floor; pair of slippers near a chair; folded reading glasses on a tray; wooden cutting board leaning against backsplash; copper kettle on stove; dish towel draped over oven handle; rumpled bedding; draped throw with visible folds-of-use.
4. **PATTERN STACKING — canonical recipe**: ONE large-scale pattern (the rug, OR the wallpaper, never both at full strength) + ONE medium-scale pattern (a pillow, a curtain) + ONE small-scale pattern (a lampshade, a piped trim, a checked accent). All three tied by sharing ONE color.
5. **WOOD-TONE MIXING — mandatory**: at least 2 distinct wood tones per scene (one lighter, one darker). Homey rooms are NEVER monochrome wood. Example combos: cerused white oak floor + dark walnut antique side table; honey-oak floor + black-stained Windsor chair; painted cabinetry + reclaimed-beam ceiling + light oak island top.
6. **OBJECT DENSITY**: every horizontal surface must hold **3-5 objects**, not 1-2 (reads vacant/gallery) and not 7+ (reads cluttered/Pottery-Barn-suburban). Consoles, mantels, coffee tables, and open shelves all follow this rule. Kitchen counters get 2-4 working-tool objects.
7. **ACCENT COLOR PULLED FROM THE HERO PRODUCT** — the room must repeat AT LEAST ONE color from the product itself (typically the metal finish and/or the dominant textile color) in at least TWO OTHER places. If product = brass + cream linen, the room contains brass pulls/lamp/picture-frame somewhere AND cream linen on curtains or upholstery somewhere. This is the single rule that most separates homey-mode from gallery-mode.
8. **ONE COLLECTED-LOOK ARTWORK CLUE** — a small framed painting (still life, landscape, portrait of an animal but NEVER a person), a vintage print, OR a hand-thrown ceramic plate hung on the wall. Never a large abstract canvas as the only artwork (that reads editorial). Gallery walls of 3-7 small works are bonus-homey.

## 3. COLOR PALETTE GUIDANCE

### Wall colors that READ homey:
- Warm cream / lime-washed off-white (Amber, EyeSwoon)
- Sage green / dusty green (Caillier, Heuman)
- Pale dusty blue / ice blue (Bartholomew, Heuman, Sikes)
- Soft blush / pale pink (Caillier, Heuman)
- Putty / warm taupe / clay plaster
- Wedgwood / china blue wallpaper (Sikes)
- Ikat or chinoiserie wallpaper (Bartholomew)
- Deep slate or petrol blue ON CABINETRY ONLY (with quiet walls)

### Wall colors that READ editorial-cold (avoid in homey mode):
- Crisp brilliant white (reads gallery)
- True gray (reads developer-spec)
- High-gloss black (reads moody-editorial, not homey)
- Pure beige with no warmth or texture
- Any single-tone wall WITHOUT visible texture (limewash, plaster, paper)

### The Accent-from-Product Rule (codified):
> Identify the product's two strongest colors (typically metal finish + dominant textile or shade color). The room must contain BOTH of these colors in at least one other element each. Brass product → brass picture-frame or pull elsewhere + the warm hue of the brass repeated in textile (mustard, ochre, brown). Cream-linen shade → cream linen on curtains, slipcover, or pillow. Polished nickel + white → quiet silver in mirror frame + white in ceramic vase.

## 4. ARCHITECTURAL TREATMENTS that read homey

- **Built-in bookshelves with books OUT OF ORDER**, mixed with framed photos, ceramics, and small framed art
- **Layered window treatments**: linen curtain over woven roman shade; OR cafe curtains; OR pinch-pleat drapery with a separate roman shade
- **Painted-and-papered walls** (wainscoting painted, upper wall papered)
- **Wainscoting / beadboard / dado rails**
- **Exposed reclaimed beams with patina** (Amber, Caillier, Stoffer, Bartholomew all use these)
- **Vertical shiplap painted in soft color** (Heuman's variant — not the bright-white Magnolia version)
- **Brick floors in kitchens** (Caillier signature)
- **Terracotta hex tile or checkerboard stone floors** (Caillier, Heuman)
- **Cottage moulding around doorways and windows** (chunky casing, not flat trim)
- **Open kitchen shelves** stacked with hand-thrown ceramics and cookbooks
- **Curved/arched doorways** with rounded plaster returns

## 5. WHAT MUST NOT APPEAR

### Universal anti-tropes (apply to BOTH minimalist and homey modes):
- No humans, no body parts, no portraits of human faces (animal portraits OK)
- No pets (cats, dogs, etc.)
- No children's toys visible as themselves
- No alcohol bottles, wine glasses, or bar setups
- No phones, laptops, tablets, TVs, modern screens
- No Chinese characters or any East Asian script
- No active food preparation (raw meat, cutting in progress)
- No religious iconography
- No brand logos

### Anti-tropes UNIQUE to homey-mode (things minimalism allows but homey forbids):
- No bright/cool fluorescent or daylight-white lighting (everything must read 2700-3000K)
- No gallery-empty walls (large blank wall above sofa = ban)
- No single-object surface styling (one vase, alone, on a console = ban — needs 3-5 objects)
- No matchy-matchy upholstery sets (sofa + 2 matching chairs in the same fabric = ban)
- No commercial florist-tight flower arrangements (must look gathered/garden-cut)
- No clutter beyond the "3-5 per surface" cap (this is mid-density, not overstuffed)

### Things minimalist mode FORBIDS but homey EXPLICITLY ALLOWS:
- Mussed/rumpled bedding (a coverlet pulled back, sheets visible)
- Throws thrown not folded
- Books face-down or stacked askew
- Mismatched cushions
- Layered rugs (rug-on-rug)
- Multiple pattern stacking
- Wear and patina on wood and metal
- Items visibly "in use" (a half-burned candle, a worn book spine)

## 6. CAMERA & FRAMING for HOMEY mode

- **Aspect**: 60% horizontal, 40% vertical. (Bartholomew/Sikes lean horizontal-symmetric; Heuman/Caillier vary.)
- **Height**: eye-level (4.5-5.5 ft) is the default. Slightly low (3-4 ft) for vertical kitchens and dining nooks.
- **Lens character**: 35mm equivalent. Slightly wider than minimalist mode (which lives at 50mm).
- **Product fills 15-25% of frame** (vs. 25-35% in minimalist mode). The room context is the subject as much as the product — pull back to show the corner the product lives in, including 2-3 other furniture pieces and at least one wall treatment.
- **Composition**: Bartholomew and Sikes lean SYMMETRIC (centered hood, flanking lanterns). Caillier and Amber lean OFF-CENTER (product 1/3 of the way in). Heuman leans on tight vignettes with one strong vertical anchor.
- **Light**: warm afternoon side-light (3-5pm equivalent), never overhead noon. Visible window light is mandatory in 80%+ of scenes. Fixtures shown ON at 2700-3000K only as accent.

## 7. THE 7 NAMED HOMEY ARCHETYPES

Pick ONE per scene. Combine ≤2 if a hybrid is requested.

### A. **Caillier-PNW-painted-cabinetry**
Sage or petrol-blue shaker cabinetry + unlacquered brass + soapstone counter + brick or terracotta hex floor + a single Persian runner + branches in earthenware. Warm whites on walls. Mood: Pacific Northwest cottage updated for a young family.

### B. **Amber-warm-California-lived-in**
Cerused white oak + black steel windows + lime-washed cream walls + faded Turkish/Persian rug + chunky earthenware with foraged branches + ONE vintage walnut antique + rust/ochre accent. Mood: married-to-the-architect Ojai/Montecito home.

### C. **Heuman-British-joyful-eclectic**
A painted-color wall (pale blue, butter yellow, sage) + fringed pleated lampshade + hand-painted ceramic plate hung as art + pattern-on-pattern (paisley + ikat + stripe) + ONE wildly saturated upholstered piece (emerald, crimson, or marigold) + brass + antique rosewood patina. Mood: West London townhouse where every room sings.

### D. **Stoffer-Michigan-family-warm-kitchen**
Deep slate-blue or hunter-green painted island + honed Carrara or Calacatta marble + brass faucet and pulls + reclaimed wood ceiling beams + open shelves with collected ceramics + vintage Persian runner on light oak floor + branches and lemons. Mood: heart-of-the-home kitchen in a 1920s renovated Michigan house.

### E. **Bartholomew-Southern-collected-traditional**
Wallpaper (ikat, chinoiserie, or chintz) + skirted upholstery with bullion fringe + blue-and-white chinoiserie vase with fresh hydrangea + layered linen curtain over roman shade + dark mahogany antique + tortoise-shell or tiger-print accent. Mood: Nashville/Charleston townhouse, fourth-generation collected.

### F. **Sikes-blue-and-white-classic**
China-blue Gracie-style wallpaper OR Wedgwood-blue painted walls + crisp white woodwork + ticking-stripe or buffalo-check upholstery + chinoiserie ceramic + a generous bouquet of white hydrangeas + polished nickel or antique brass. Mood: Hollywood-Hills-meets-Hamptons.

### G. **EyeSwoon-curated-artisan** (homey-leaning-editorial)
Limewashed plaster walls + Calacatta marble + matte black or deep navy cabinetry + sculptural curly-willow branches in a hand-thrown matte black vessel + 3-object styled shelves + a single ceramic plate hung as art. Mood: Brooklyn-brownstone-renovated-by-an-artist. Use this when the product itself is contemporary and the request is "homey but elevated."

## 8. APPLICATION CHECKLIST (paste into LLM prompt)

When generating a homey scene, verify ALL of these are present in the prompt:

- [ ] Archetype named (one of A-G above)
- [ ] Wall treatment specified (color + finish, e.g., "lime-washed warm cream plaster")
- [ ] ≥2 wood tones called out
- [ ] ≥3 textile layers on primary seating (upholstery + 2 patterned pillows + thrown throw)
- [ ] Branch arrangement OR potted plant OR fresh-cut bouquet, in named ceramic/earthenware vessel
- [ ] ≥1 "just-here" cue from the list
- [ ] Pattern recipe: 1 large + 1 medium + 1 small, sharing one tied color
- [ ] 3-5 objects on every visible horizontal surface
- [ ] ≥2 accent points repeating colors from the hero product
- [ ] ≥1 small-scale framed artwork (still life, animal portrait, landscape, ceramic plate)
- [ ] One architectural homey-cue (beam, wainscoting, brick/terracotta floor, wallpaper, built-in shelves)
- [ ] Camera at eye-level, 35mm, product fills 15-25% of frame
- [ ] Warm 2700-3000K light, side-light from a visible window
- [ ] All universal anti-tropes excluded (no humans, pets, alcohol, screens, Chinese, food prep)
- [ ] Lived-in cues affirmatively allowed (rumpled, thrown, mussed, askew)
